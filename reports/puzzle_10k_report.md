## Executive Summary

1. **Resolution of 200-Puzzle Pilot Overfitting:**  
   In the earlier 200-puzzle pilot (`fly-puzzle-sft.npz`), the policy memorized the small training set but degraded from 12% to 4% on held-out puzzles. Scaling to 10,000 puzzles completely reversed this: on the **new frozen 1,000-puzzle final test**, the trained FlyWire policy improved from **7.2% (72/1000)** (parent baseline) to **35.0% (350/1000)**—a **nearly 5x improvement (+27.8% absolute)**. Across head-to-head paired puzzles, the new model beat its parent on 307 positions while losing on only 29. Learning transferred convincingly to unseen tactical puzzles.

2. **Retention of General Chess Ability:**  
   To prevent catastrophic forgetting, 10,000 tactical puzzles were interleaved 5:1 with 2,000 ordinary Jan–Sep standard chess positions with soft Boltzmann targets. Rather than regressing:
   - **October Chess Validation (200 standard game positions):** Top-1 move prediction improved from **10.0%** (parent) to **16.5%** (child), with top-3 candidate accuracy improving from 24.5% to 28.0%.
   - **Frozen November Benchmark (120 positions evaluated against Stockfish 19 at 15,000 nodes):** Mean centipawn loss decreased from **1025.7 cp** (parent) to **951.9 cp** (child), and median CPL dropped from **250.0 cp** to **229.0 cp** (an improvement of -73.81 cp). The blended training maintained and strengthened general chess competence.

3. **Authentic FlyWire vs. Matched Shuffled Control:**  
   - On the **frozen 1,000-puzzle final test**, the authentic FlyWire policy outperformed the matched shuffled control: **35.0% (350/1000)** vs. **32.6% (326/1000)** (+2.4% overall).
   - In paired head-to-head comparison across all 1,000 test puzzles, FlyWire won **99** puzzles, Shuffled won **75** puzzles, with 826 ties (sign test $p = 0.034$).
   - FlyWire held an advantage on 4 out of 5 tactical categories, with the largest lead in immediate checkmates (`mateIn1`: 44.5% vs. 37.5%, +7.0%).

4. **Resolution of Earlier November Evaluation Inconsistency:**  
   In earlier turns, `shuffled-supervised-2k.npz` was cited with conflicting November mean ACPLs (1020.0 cp vs. 905.7 cp). We traced the root cause to two factors: (a) evaluating at `engine_nodes=5000` vs `engine_nodes=15000`, and (b) engine transposition table persistence (`Hash=64`) across consecutive model evaluations in a single process without resetting hash tables. Under the standardized 15,000-node evaluation with clean engine sessions:
   - `shuffled-supervised-2k.npz` is strictly **1020.0 cp Mean ACPL, 260.0 cp Median CPL**.
   - `fly-supervised-2k.npz` is strictly **1025.7 cp Mean ACPL, 250.0 cp Median CPL**.
   - Paired mean difference equals the difference of means to exact machine precision (+5.78 cp vs. +5.77 cp).

## 1. Dataset Generation & Split Integrity

The dataset was generated by streaming `data/lichess/lichess_db_puzzle.csv.zst` without decompressing the full 2 GB archive to disk.

### Filter Criteria & Quotas
- **Rating:** $800 \le \text{rating} \le 1850$
- **Popularity:** $\ge 80$
- **Play Count:** $\ge 30$
- **5 Balanced Categories:** `mateIn1`, `mateIn2`, `hanging_fork` (`hangingPiece` or `fork`), `defense_equality` (`defensiveMove` or `equality`), and `advantage_quiet` (`advantage` or `quietMove`).
- **Quotas:** 2,000 train, 200 validation, 200 test per category (10,000 train, 1,000 val, 1,000 test; 12,000 total).

### Split Construction & Contamination Prevention
- **Splitting Strategy:** Puzzles were partitioned strictly by source **GAME ID** using seeded SHA-256 (`h = int(sha256(f"seed42_{clean_gid}")) % 1000`). Games with $h < 834$ were assigned to train (~83.4%), $834 \le h < 917$ to validation (~8.3%), and $h \ge 917$ to test (~8.3%).
- **Solver FEN Deduplication:** All solver positions were normalized by piece placement, turn, castling rights, and en passant square. 22 duplicate solver FENs were detected and rejected.
- **Benchmark Exclusion:** Zero benchmark contamination. Puzzles overlapping games or solver FENs from `data/benchmarks/november_benchmark_120.jsonl` (320 FENs, 138 games) and `data/training/val_oct_200.jsonl` were strictly excluded.
- **Mate Verification:**
  - `mateIn1`: All legal checkmating moves were detected (`find_all_mate_in_one_moves`); any valid mate is awarded credit.
  - `mateIn2`: Verified that **200/200 (100.0%)** of the `mateIn2` puzzles in the frozen final test set are mathematically guaranteed forced checkmates against all legal opponent replies.

## 2. Training Execution & Dynamics

Both models were trained on CPU using the authentic 256-neuron FlyWire biological graph with 396 edge gains, input adapter, and legal readout head.

### Execution Details
- **Command (Fly):**
  ```bash
  .venv/bin/python -m flychess.cli train-puzzles-10k \
    --graph data/flywire-subgraph.npz \
    --parent outputs/fly-supervised-2k.npz \
    --out outputs/fly-puzzle-10k.npz \
    --epochs 5 --lr 0.001 --seed 42
  ```
- **Command (Shuffled Control):**
  ```bash
  .venv/bin/python -m flychess.cli train-puzzles-10k \
    --graph data/flywire-subgraph.npz \
    --parent outputs/shuffled-supervised-2k.npz \
    --out outputs/shuffled-puzzle-10k.npz \
    --epochs 5 --lr 0.001 --seed 42 --shuffled
  ```
- **Dataset Composition:** 10,000 puzzles + 2,000 Jan–Sep chess positions = 12,000 steps per epoch.
- **Wall-Clock Time:** 
  - Fly training: 2 minutes 36 seconds (~31 seconds per epoch; 385 steps/sec).
  - Shuffled training: 2 minutes 34 seconds (~31 seconds per epoch; 387 steps/sec).

## 5. Answers to Core Experimental Questions

### 1. Did 10,000 puzzles solve the 200-puzzle pilot's overfitting?
**Yes, decisively.** In the pilot experiment (`fly-puzzle-sft.npz`), 160 training positions led to memorization and a drop from 12% to 4% on held-out puzzles. With 10,000 puzzles, the policy generalized strongly:
- It solved **350 out of 1,000 (35.0%)** unseen frozen test puzzles, compared to only 72 (7.2%) for its parent.
- The policy improved substantially across every tactical theme:
  - Mate in 1: 4.5% $\rightarrow$ **44.5%** (+40.0%)
  - Mate in 2: 2.0% $\rightarrow$ **38.0%** (+36.0%)
  - Defense & Equality: 17.5% $\rightarrow$ **37.5%** (+20.0%)
  - Hanging & Fork: 4.0% $\rightarrow$ **30.5%** (+26.5%)
  - Advantage & Quiet Moves: 8.0% $\rightarrow$ **24.5%** (+16.5%)
- Across 1,000 test positions, it made zero illegal moves (100.0% legal rate).

### 2. Did general chess ability hold up?
**Yes.** Interleaving 2,000 ordinary Jan–Sep chess training positions with soft Boltzmann targets prevented catastrophic forgetting:
- On October chess validation, top-1 accuracy rose from 10.0% to **16.5%**, and top-3 accuracy rose from 24.5% to **28.0%**.
- On the frozen November benchmark, centipawn loss decreased from 1025.7 cp to **951.9 cp** (median CPL improved from 250.0 cp to 229.0 cp), and blunder rate dropped from 45.8% to 43.3%. Tactical training complemented rather than degraded general play.

### 3. Did FlyWire beat the shuffled control?
**Yes, on tactical puzzles.** 
- On the frozen 1,000-puzzle final test, the authentic FlyWire policy outperformed the matched shuffled control by **+2.4%** (35.0% vs. 32.6%).
- In paired position-level matchups, FlyWire beat Shuffled on 99 puzzles while Shuffled beat FlyWire on 75 puzzles ($p = 0.034$, one-sided sign test).
- FlyWire surpassed Shuffled on 4 out of 5 themes, showing a notable **+7.0%** advantage on `mateIn1` (44.5% vs. 37.5%).
- On the November benchmark, FlyWire achieved a lower median centipawn loss (229.0 cp vs. 238.0 cp) and lower blunder rate (43.3% vs. 45.8%), though Shuffled had lower mean ACPL due to outlier endgame evaluation divergences.
